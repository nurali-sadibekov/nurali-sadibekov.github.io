What I value about engineering is that it's an independent view on life itself. It's an independent angle, from which you can observe nature differently. Taking any natural phenomena — you have a better understanding not only the physics behind it, but, in my case, understanding that there's something deeper than the first impression. There's a reason for something happening. Nothing happens without a reason. 

Even though I might not work as an engineer in the future, it will still be my background. Part of my development. If I will not be an engineer, I will be someone who values it and is not blind to it. During my BEng. of New Energy Science and Engineering program, I had a chance to eliminate my initial thought on engineering as a field. It was something looking easy at first, until you start to understand that it's a huge field with lots of variables moving around. That's the importance and value of having an engineering degree, at least for me. 

I am not an A-grade student, and most of the engineering subjects are challenging for me. But it still has value when you learn part of it. Just like a book — you might not fully understand the full meaning behind the word, but it will leave a mark on you.
